## What it does
Decapitator is an analog-saturation modeler: it pushes audio through one of five carefully-modeled vintage hardware paths (tape preamp, console channels, studio tube/pentode distortion units) to add harmonics — from subtle warmth and "beef" to all-out screaming distortion. Unlike static convolution-style saturation, the models are dynamic and respond to the program material (you hear it react to transients and instrument dynamics). A pre-saturation Tone/Low Cut/Thump section shapes what frequencies get distorted, a post-saturation High Cut tames "fizz," the Punish button slams an extra +20 dB into the circuit, and a wet/dry Mix knob gives built-in parallel processing. Distinct for: musical, gear-specific harmonic flavors; the Punish "beautiful agony"; and full pre/post tonal sculpting in one box.

## Controls (every param → musical effect)
| control | range / unit | what it does | when to reach for it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Style** | A / E / N / T / P (5) | Selects the saturation algorithm = which modeled hardware drives the sound. **A** = Ampex 350 tape-drive preamp (ultra-ultra-smooth tube distortion). **E** = Chandler/EMI TG console channel (beefy low end + smooth airy top). **N** = Neve 1057 input channel (early Germanium-transistor Neve; weighty solid lows, focused mids, distinctive esp. on guitars). **T** = Thermionic Culture Vulture *Triode* (warm, punchy, even-harmonic dirt — great on drums/percussion). **P** = Culture Vulture *Pentode* (odd-harmonic distortion, different/harder character than triode). | Pick the color first: A for gentle tape warmth; E/N for console body; T for warm even-harmonic grit; P for aggressive odd-harmonic edge. |
| **Drive** | 0–10 | Main input gain into the saturation circuit — the harder it's pushed, the more it saturates. Distortion character is style-dependent. Also raises output level. | Primary saturation amount. Low for subtle warmth, high to push gear "into the red." |
| **Punish** | On / Off button | Adds an extra **+20 dB** of gain into the signal path on top of Drive — loud, brutal, heavily distorted; each style "screams in agony" differently. Also raises output level. | When normal Drive isn't enough — extreme/destructive distortion on vocals, drums, guitars, synths. |
| **Attitude Meter** | VU display (≈ -20…+3) | Highly responsive VU-style meter (Simpson VU / Ampex 35X inspired) showing level *in relation to Drive* — a visual guide to how hard you're driving. **Does NOT measure output.** | Watch it to gauge how hard the saturation stage is being hit. |
| **Thump** | On / Off toggle | Adds a few dB of low-frequency boost right at the Low Cut frequency — emulates analog tape "head bump," makes things fat. Boosts the lows *before* the saturation circuit. | Fatten the low end / add tape-style thickness; pairs with Low Cut frequency. Can add flab if overused. |
| **Low Cut** | 20 Hz – 1 kHz | High-pass filter applied **pre-saturation** — removes lows before they hit the circuit to prevent "flabbiness" when distorting bass-heavy material. Sets the Thump frequency. | Tighten before distortion; high settings (with High Cut) give lo-fi telephone/AM-radio effects. |
| **Tone** | Dark ↔ Bright | Gentle sloping tilt EQ applied **pre-saturation**. Dark = boost lows / cut highs; Bright = cut lows / boost highs. Because it's pre-saturation it changes *which frequencies distort* — dramatic effect. | Shape the color of the distortion itself; tilt darker for warmth, brighter for edge/presence. |
| **High Cut** | 1 kHz – 20 kHz | Low-pass filter applied **post-saturation** — removes high frequencies / "fizz" generated by the distortion (emulates analog output-transformer / tape HF loss). | Tame harshness after saturating; retain analog character without excess top. |
| **Steep** | On / Off toggle | Sets High Cut slope: Off = gentle 6 dB/oct; On = super-steep 30 dB/oct. | Steep + High Cut ~4–5 kHz emulates a guitar-amp speaker cabinet / direct-to-board guitar tone. |
| **Output** | -24 dB … 0 dB | Master output level (post everything) — decouples saturation amount from final volume. | Compensate for Drive/Punish gain; gain-stage manually when Auto is off. |
| **Auto** | On / Off (Auto-Gain) | When on, takes over the Output knob and automatically turns output down as Drive increases (Output moves opposite to Drive) for roughly level-matched gain staging. | Audition Drive amounts without volume jumps; quick level-matched A/B. |
| **Mix** | Dry ↔ Wet (0–100%) | Blends unprocessed (dry) signal with the saturated (wet) signal — built-in parallel processing, no routing/sub-mix needed. | Parallel saturation: restore transients chopped off by distortion (like parallel compression) on drums/bus; dial in subtle character on full mixes. |

## Use by lens
- **Producer (create):** A sound-design distortion box. Pick a Style for vibe (A tape, T/P tube fuzz), crank Drive + Punish for aggressive synth/vocal/drum textures, use Tone pre-saturation to morph the distortion's character, and Low Cut + High Cut (with Steep) for lo-fi telephone/amp/AM-radio effects. Great for "anything that sounds a little too polite."
- **Mixing (balance):** Everyday harmonic enhancer on individual tracks — gentle Drive for analog warmth/glue, Thump to fatten, Tone to color, High Cut to keep it smooth. Use Mix for parallel saturation to add weight/presence while keeping transients (esp. drums, bass, vocals). Auto-Gain keeps A/B honest.
- **Mastering (finalize):** Use with restraint on the bus — very low Drive (often Style A or E for smoothness), Auto-Gain on, and dial back with the Mix knob for subtle parallel coloration / "analog console" glue. Watch that Drive adds level and the High Cut shaping is gentle. (Stereo-link/true-stereo behavior is the v5 plug-in default; no mid/side controls.)

## Notes / gotchas
- **Signal flow matters:** Tone, Low Cut, Thump act **before** saturation (they change what distorts); High Cut + Steep act **after** saturation (they tame the result). Output and Auto are at the very end.
- **Punish = +20 dB** hard gain — expect big level jumps; use Output or Auto.
- **Attitude Meter shows Drive level, not output** — don't use it for output metering.
- Drive (and Punish) increase output level; enable **Auto** or pull **Output** down to gain-stage.
- Models are dynamic/program-dependent — the same settings react differently to different sources.
- No oversampling / latency / sidechain controls exposed in the manual; CPU is light. Part of the Soundtoys 5 bundle (works inside Effect Rack with other Soundtoys).
- Styles named A/E/N/T/P after Ampex / EMI / Neve / Triode / Pentode hardware (Soundtoys' modeled references, not affiliated/endorsed).
