# Composition Methods

> Composition is a sequence of decisions about what to repeat, what to change, and when to surprise. There are only 12 notes — the music lives in *which note next, and how long until the listener gets it.*

> Rule-of-thumb voice: tables below are rules for Easby to apply, not first-person narration.

---

## Melodic Development — 6 moves I always have available

| Move | What it does | When to reach for it |
|---|---|---|
| **Sequence** | Repeat motif starting on a different scale degree | Extending a strong hook without repetition fatigue |
| **Inversion** | Flip melodic contour upside-down | Need a fresh angle on a known phrase |
| **Retrograde** | Play the phrase backwards | Bridge sections, transitions (✗ rarely works in tonal music — see `06-music-theory.md` §5 for constraint) |
| **Augmentation** | Stretch durations 2× or 4× | Make an idea feel epic, slow it for a climax |
| **Diminution** | Compress durations ÷2 or ÷4 | Energy ramp, fill section |
| **Fragmentation** | Take 2–3 notes from phrase, develop alone | Verse from chorus, ambient sections |

⚡ **A great melody usually contains its own development.** Look at the 8-bar hook and ask: which 2 notes could sequence into the next 8?

---

## Rhythmic Development

- **Shift by 16th** — phrase displaced one 16th note → polyrhythmic feel, same notes.
- **Hemiola** — imply 3-beat groupings inside 4/4 (3+3+2 or 3+3+3+3+4). The reason "On The Floor" feels off-grid.
- **Add/remove one note** from a repeating pattern → evolving pattern without new material.
- **Displace kick or snare by one 16th** → groove shift. The trap-vs-house line is here.

⚡ Rhythmic displacement is the cheapest way to add interest. Free move, always on the table.

## Harmony & Chord Variation

| Technique | What it does | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| **Deceptive cadence** | Replace final I with vi (or bVI in minor) | Surprise resolution — "wait, it didn't land" |
| **Secondary dominants** | V7 of the next chord (A7 → D in C major) | Forward momentum, jazz/Beatles colour |
| **Borrowed chords (modal mixture)** | bVII, bVI from parallel minor/major | Cinematic, "movie trailer" warmth |
| **Tritone substitution** | Replace V7 with bII7 (same tritone, different bass) | Smooth chromatic bass motion, jazz cliché in best sense |
| **Add extensions** | Triads → 7ths, 9ths, sus2, sus4 | Colour without changing function |

⚡ **Voicing matters more than chord choice.** A C-major triad in close root position vs spread inversion are different emotional events.

## Texture & Arrangement

### The 4 Contrast Levers

1. **Density** — full → sparse → full. Ears reset during sparse sections. Sparse beats are not weakness; they're setup.
2. **Register** — all-high section followed by all-low = instant drama. Mid-register fatigue is real.
3. **Tension via unresolved dissonance** — tritone or major 7th interval hanging unresolved. Pay it off later.
4. **Call and response** — melodic question left, answer right. Works in stereo, works across instruments.

### Arrangement Is Subtractive

⚡ Start with everything playing. Remove until it breathes. Never start sparse and add — that route ends up dense by accident.

### The Story Curve

| Section | Function | Density |
|---|---|---|
| Intro | Promise | Sparse, hint at hook |
| Verse | Setup | Medium, leave room for vocal |
| Pre-chorus | Lift | Add layers, ramp tension |
| Chorus | Payoff | Full, deliver hook fully |
| Verse 2 | Setup again, but transformed | Medium + new element |
| Bridge | Surprise / contrast | Different texture or key |
| Final chorus | Bigger payoff | Full + ornament |
| Outro | Release | Subtractive fade or hard stop |

⚡ **Intention + surprise = interesting music.** All intention = boring; all surprise = noise. Each section should be predictable enough to nod to and surprising enough to lean in.

## DeSantis / Ableton — 74 strategies, condensed

The pattern across all of them:

- **Constrain changes:** change rhythm but not pitch, OR pitch but not rhythm. Never both at once.
- **Limitations force creativity.** Remove one element; see if it's missed.
- **Automation tells the story.** Sounds should evolve, not loop statically.
- **When starting:** copy a structure that works and re-fill it with new material.
- **When stuck mid-track:** delete the last thing added; listen to what's left.
- **When finishing:** stop adding; arrange what's there.

### Problem-stage taxonomy (DeSantis's organizing axis)

DeSantis groups the 74 strategies into Problems of Beginning / Progressing / Finishing. Easby maps an operator's "I'm stuck" to which stage they're in:

| Stage | Symptom | DeSantis solution family | Easby variation move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beginning** | Blank slate paralysis, "what should this be?" | Copy a structure that works, then re-fill with new material | Suggest a known-good genre template (`03d-genre-templates.md`), let operator fill cells |
| **Progressing** | Loop drag, variation fatigue, "this section won't end" | Automation + constraint swap (change one parameter, hold others) | Emit amt 2–3 variation that changes exactly one axis (timbre OR rhythm OR pitch) |
| **Finishing** | Decision paralysis, "is this done?" | Stop adding, arrange what's there. Accept good enough, ship | Refuse further variation requests; emit `Refusal {reason: "stage:finishing", redirect: "arrange existing material"}` |

⚡ When operator says "I'm stuck," ask first: *which stage?* Then pick the right solution family.

## Iterative Layering — composition order from scratch

> Canonical here. `00-producer-mind.md` references this section; the taste rule "foundation first, pads last" lives there. Do NOT duplicate these 6 steps elsewhere.

1. **Rhythm only.** Groove first. No pitch decisions yet.
2. **Bass.** Defines harmonic rhythm. Lock kick/bass note-length relationship.
3. **Chords.** Confirm harmony. Voicing matters more than choice.
4. **Melody.** Write above locked foundation.
5. **Texture + atmosphere.** Seasoning, last.
6. **Subtract.** Remove anything not serving 1–4.

✗ Composers who start with the melody usually end up with a melody floating over no foundation. The melody adapts to the groove, not the other way around.

## The Constraint Recipe (Huang)

Pick one session constraint and stick to it:

- One instrument, one BPM, one key.
- No reverb/delay allowed.
- Must finish in 1 hour.
- 4 sounds maximum.
- 8 bars only.
- No edits — first take of everything stays.

⚡ Constraints surface what the producer actually knows vs. what's been hidden behind complexity. They also kill choice paralysis.

## Linking composition to synthesis

| Composition need | Synthesis answer |
|---|---|
| Need foundation, low movement | Sub bass — sine + sub layer (`02-sound-design-recipes.md`) |
| Need rhythmic energy without new notes | Pluck synth with high resonance, retrigger pattern |
| Need lift into chorus | Riser using filter sweep on noise + pitch envelope |
| Need surprise harmonic colour | FM bell on a single chord tone (non-integer C:M) |
| Need space between sections | Subtractive arrangement; pad with long release for tail |
| Need tension during pre-chorus | Unresolved 7th in chords + filter sweep up + reduce kick |

⚡ Every composition decision has a synthesis or mix consequence. They're not separate steps — they're one decision tree.

---

## Linking composition to recording

| Composition decision | Recording consequence |
|---|---|
| Sparse arrangement | Need cleaner recordings; room mics matter more |
| Stacked vocals | Plan mic technique for stackability (see `04-recording-production.md`) |
| Acoustic instrument as lead | Capture room and detail mics from the start |
| Dense electronic arrangement | Less room reverb needed in capture; close-mic everything |
| Stereo width is the point | Capture in stereo at source (Blumlein, X-Y) |
