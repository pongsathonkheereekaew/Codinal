## 10. Modulation — Changing Key Without Killing the Loop

| Type | How | Risk | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pivot chord** | Find a chord shared by both keys, reinterpret it | Low | Smooth verse-to-bridge moves. The default. |
| **Direct (phrase) modulation** | Just start the new key, no prep | Medium | "Truck driver" key change in last chorus. Cheap but effective. |
| **Common-tone** | Hold one pitch across the modulation, reharmonize around it | Low | Subtle, classical. Strauss, film score. |
| **Sequential** | ⚡ Repeat a phrase a step (or 3rd, or 5th) higher | Lowest | The safest, most musical modulation for loop variation. Same shape, new altitude. |
| **Chromatic pivot** | Use a chord that functions in neither key but bridges them (often Ger+6 or dim7) | High | Jazz, advanced reharmonization. One per track. |

⚡ **Sequential modulation is the variation engine's best friend.** Take the loop, repeat it transposed +2 semitones (whole step) or +3 (minor 3rd). The ear hears development, not modulation. No pivot chord, no transition — the sequence IS the transition.
