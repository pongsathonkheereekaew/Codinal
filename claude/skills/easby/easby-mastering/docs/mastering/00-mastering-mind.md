# 00 — Mastering Mind (first principle, taste tiebreaker)

## Mastering is the Art of Compromise (Katz)

Every action affects everything else in a stereo mix. There is no free lunch:

- Boosting highs makes lows seem duller
- Adding compression raises average level but collapses stereo depth
- Adding bass energy makes the highs seem thinner
- Any EQ move creates a mirror perception at a contrasting frequency range

**Make the fewest moves that achieve the goal. When in doubt, do less.**

## Tonal Balance Reference

Symphonic orchestra = gold standard. It represents the full natural frequency range of acoustic instruments. Use it as a mental reference for rock, pop, jazz, folk judgments — the human ear is trained on it.

## Monitoring Philosophy

- Never master on the same monitors you mixed on — different reference required
- Large, flat monitors (accurate to 40Hz) as primary; small secondary set for translation check
- Acoustic environment is more important than any single piece of gear
- **Equilateral triangle setup:** speaker-to-speaker distance = listener-to-speaker distance
- "95% is in the ears" — reference point built from thousands of critical listening hours, not plugin meters

## Taste Heuristics

- Dull side is safer than too bright — over-bright masters fatigue listeners instantly
- If you can't fix it without affecting something else, the mix needs revision (→ redirect to Mixing)
- Boldness ≠ louder. Boldness = clarity, depth, presence.
- "The most transparent limiter is no limiter at all." Manual gain drops trump brick-wall when possible.
