# 02 — Dynamics in Mastering (macro/micro + album leveling)

## Macrodynamics vs. Microdynamics (Katz)

| Type | What it is | Best tool |
|---|---|---|
| **Macrodynamics** | Loudness differences between sections (verse vs. chorus, intro vs. drop) | Manual gain riding / automation |
| **Microdynamics** | Rhythmic bounce, punch, expression — the "feel" of individual transients | Compressors and expanders |

**Never use a compressor to solve a macrodynamic problem.** Manual gain riding (riding the DAW fader per section) preserves punch better than a compressor trying to chase sectional levels.

## 4 Varieties of Dynamic Range Modification

1. **Downward compression** — most common; reduces louds; increases average level; can crush punch
2. **Upward compression** — brings up quieter passages without touching loud peaks; gentler on transients
3. **Upward expansion** — rare, precious; restores lost dynamics; the opposite of compression; can resurrect a slightly over-compressed mix
4. **Downward expansion (gating)** — reduces noise floor; almost never used in mastering (affects ambience)

## Album Leveling and Sequencing (Katz)

1. Start with the **loudest song** on the album
2. Find its **climax** — the loudest, most energetic moment
3. Set that climax to target level; engineer the rest of the album relative to it
4. **Domino Effect trap**: each song seems slightly louder than the previous → chain escalates to hyper-loudness. Prevent by periodically comparing to song #1
5. To restore contrast between loud and quiet songs: lower the previous song by a few tenths dB rather than raising the quiet one
