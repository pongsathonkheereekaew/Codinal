## Key Techniques Glossary

**Shuffle:** Hi-hat on 1st and 3rd note of each triplet (skipping the middle). Creates swung feel.
**Half-time shuffle:** Shuffle hi-hat but snare only on beat 3 (Rosanna Shuffle, Fool In The Rain).
**Ghost notes:** Played very quietly, typically in brackets in notation. Fill space without dominating.
**Feathering:** Bass drum played very lightly (heel-down technique) — more felt than heard.
**One drop:** Reggae — bass drum replaces snare on beat 3 (or 2&4). Beat 1 often skipped.
**Linear drumming:** No two limbs play simultaneously. Common in progressive metal (Dance Of Eternity).
**Double bass sixteenth notes:** Both feet alternating RL to fill 16th grid — thrash metal signature.
**Quarter note triplets:** Three evenly spaced notes across two beats = "triplet feel" against straight count.
**Polyrhythm:** Two different rhythmic cycles simultaneously (e.g., quarter note triplets vs quarter notes).
**Flam:** Two nearly-simultaneous notes — grace note from one hand, then main note from other.
**Buzz/press roll:** Stick pressed into drum, bounces multiple times = buzzing sound.
**Heel-toe technique:** One foot produces two BD notes — heel hits pedal, then toe rocks forward.
**Displaced backbeat:** Snare moved from traditional 2&4 to unexpected position (e.g., '+' of 2 or 4).
**Swung/shuffled 16ths:** 16th notes unevenly spaced — 'e' and 'a' positions played slightly late. Standard in funk and soul.
**Open hi-hat stab:** Hi-hat struck while foot releases to open cymbal — creates "chick" or wash sound.
**Cross-stick:** Drumstick laid across snare head, butt end tapped on rim. Quieter, woody sound.
