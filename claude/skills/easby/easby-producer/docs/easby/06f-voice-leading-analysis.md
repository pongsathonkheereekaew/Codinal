## 21. Voice Leading Rules — Part Writing [Kostka/Payne Ch. 5–9, reinforcing]

These rules apply to all 4-part SATB writing and inform Easby's slice-stacking decisions.

### Spacing
- **Closed position**: S, A, T within an octave.
- **Open position**: S-A or A-T gap exceeds an octave.
- **Rule**: S-A interval ≤ P8; A-T interval ≤ P8; T-B interval: no limit but avoid extreme register.
- No more than a P8 between any two adjacent upper voices (S-A, A-T). T-B can exceed P8.

### Doubling Rules
- **Root position triad**: double the root. If can't, double 5th. Never double the leading tone (7̂).
- **First inversion (6)**: double the soprano note (top voice), or root if soprano is the 3rd.
- **vii°6**: double the 3rd (never the bass note, which is the leading tone).
- **Second inversion (6/4)**: double the bass (= the 5th of the chord).

### Forbidden Parallels
- **Parallel perfect 5ths (P5)**: two voices moving from P5 to P5 in same direction. Forbidden.
- **Parallel perfect octaves (P8)**: same. Forbidden.
- **Consecutive unisons**: forbidden.
- **Hidden (direct) 5ths/8ths**: outer voices (S and B) move in same direction to P5 or P8, with soprano moving by leap. Forbidden (also called "exposed" parallels).

### Voice Leading Motion Types
- **Contrary motion**: voices move in opposite directions. Most independence. Preferred.
- **Oblique motion**: one voice holds, other moves.
- **Similar motion**: both move same direction but different intervals.
- **Parallel motion**: both move same direction, same interval.

### Melodic Rules for Each Voice
- Avoid augmented intervals melodically (especially A2, A4).
- Large leaps (6th+) should be preceded and followed by contrary stepwise motion.
- Never leap from/to a diminished interval.
- Avoid two successive leaps in the same direction unless they outline a triad.
- Leading tone (7̂) resolves up to tonic (1̂) — "frustrated" leading tone (resolves down to 5̂) only acceptable when inner voice and V is full (has 5th present).

### Six-Four Chord Rules (2nd Inversion — "6/4")
Three types, each with strict approach/leave requirements:

| Type | Context | Approach | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cadential** | Before V at cadence | Any | Both upper voices step down to V chord (4-3, 6-5 motion) |
| **Passing** | Between I and I6 (or vice versa) | Stepwise bass | Bass continues stepwise; inner voices may move by step |
| **Pedal** | Embellishes a chord (I → I6/4 → I) | Bass held or returns | Bass returns to same note; voices ornament and return |

## 22. Extended Harmonic Analysis Methods [Kostka/Payne]

### Labeling Chromatic Chords
1. **Roman numeral** with accidentals: bVI, bIII, N (Neapolitan), It+6, Fr+6, Ger+6.
2. **Parenthetical secondary function**: V/V, vii°7/iv, etc.
3. **Common-tone diminished**: ct°7 (parenthesized — no Roman numeral, no root function).
4. **Simultaneity/coloristic chord**: root name + chord type only (e.g., "Ab major" or "Db7") — no Roman numeral when no functional relationship.
5. **Chromatic mediant**: bracket the chord with a CM label.
6. **Polychord**: fraction notation F/C.
7. **Set theory prime form**: [0,1,6] for post-tonal.

### Concentric vs. Non-concentric Analysis
After full analysis, determine: does the piece open and close in the same key? If yes = concentric. If no = non-concentric (common in Mahler, Wolf, late Romantic).

### Expanded Tonality Checklist (post-Romantic analysis)
Ask in order:
1. Does the piece have a tonal center? (Yes/No)
2. If yes, how is it established? (repetition, bass, cadential approach, return at end)
3. How is it avoided? (deceptive cadence, modal mixture, chromatic mediant, sequence, double chromatic mediant)
4. What other procedures mark it as late Romantic? (planing, chain of V7s, omnibus, whole-tone passages)

## 23. Historical Style Fingerprints [Kostka/Payne Ch. 27–28]

| Era / Style | Key fingerprints | Easby application |
|---|---|---|
| **Late Romantic** | Chain of dominant 7ths, mediant key areas, NCTs blur harmony, avoided V-I cadences, chromatic contrapuntal lines | Use chromatic mediant shifts, suppress resolution, add NCT density |
| **Impressionism (Debussy/Ravel)** | Plagal cadence, mediant harmonic relationships, pentatonic melody, parallel chords (planing), whole-tone passages | Diatonic planing on variation, pentatonic pitch selection, avoid leading tone |
| **Expressionism / 12-tone** | Avoid triads, avoid octave doubling, wide leaps, tone row transformations | Atonal variation: pitch-shift by set-theoretic interval classes |
| **Bartók / Eastern European** | Octatonic scale, folk modes (Dorian, Lydian), bitonality, asymmetric meter, additive rhythm | Octatonic pitch selection, asymmetric loop lengths |
| **Stravinsky / Neoclassical** | Pandiatonicism, polychords, polytonal passages, strong rhythmic drive, metric shifts | Pandiatonic reharmonization, polyrhythm layering |
| **Minimalism** | Phase displacement, gradual process, diatonic or pentatonic materials, extreme repetition | Phase offset variation (loop start point shifted), minimal pitch variation |

## 24. Voice Leading Rules [Kostka/Payne Ch. 4–5]

These rules govern how individual lines move within and between chords. They sit underneath every part-writing decision in § 25 and refine the broader voice leading already sketched in § 21.

### Melodic Line — 5 Rules

1. **Rhythm.** Keep rhythm simple. Most durations ≥ one beat. Final note lands on a strong beat.
2. **Harmony.** Every melody note must belong to the chord harmonizing it (NCTs handled separately — see `06a-core-progressions.md` §4).
3. **Contour.** Melody primarily conjunct (stepwise). A single focal point — the highest note of the line — anchors the shape.
4. **Leaps.**
   - 4a. Avoid augmented intervals, 7ths, and any interval > P8. Diminished intervals allowed only if the melody immediately changes direction by step after the leap.
   - 4b. Any interval > P4: approach AND leave in the direction OPPOSITE to the leap.
   - 4c. Consecutive leaps in the same direction must outline a triad.
5. **Tendency tones.**
   - 7̂ has a strong upward pull to 1̂. Exception: the descending line 1̂–7̂–6̂–5̂ is acceptable.
   - 4̂ tends down to 3̂ (less obligatory than 7̂→1̂).

### Voicing Conventions

- **Close structure:** less than an octave between soprano and tenor.
- **Open structure:** an octave or more between soprano and tenor.
- **Spacing rule:** Adjacent upper parts (excluding bass) must stay within an octave. S–A ≤ P8; A–T ≤ P8. T–B may exceed an octave.
- **No crossed voices:** no part crosses above soprano or below bass. Alto and tenor may cross briefly.

**Vocal ranges (SATB):** Soprano C4–G5, Alto G3–C5, Tenor C3–G4, Bass E2–C4.

### Five Motion Types (Between Any Two Voices)

| Type | Definition |
|---|---|
| Static | Neither part moves |
| Oblique | Only one part moves |
| Contrary | Both move in opposite directions |
| Similar | Both move same direction, different intervals |
| Parallel | Both move same direction, same interval |

### Parallel / Contrary / Direct Interval Rules

⚡ **Parallel P5s → FORBIDDEN.** Two voices a P5 apart moving to new pitch classes that are also a P5 apart.
⚡ **Parallel P8s / octaves → FORBIDDEN.**
**Parallel 4ths, 3rds, 6ths → ALLOWED.**

⚡ **Contrary 5ths/8ths → also avoided.** Do NOT "fix" parallel 5ths by inverting one voice an octave — that creates contrary 5ths, equally poor.

**Unequal 5ths:** P5 followed by °5 (or reverse) in the same two voices.
- °5→P5 between bass and any upper voice → AVOID.
- °5→P5 or P5→°5 between upper voices only → acceptable.

**Direct (hidden) 5ths/8ths:** outer parts (soprano + bass) move in the same direction into a P5 or P8, with a leap in the soprano. AVOID when soprano leaps into the P5 or P8. Exception: soprano moves by step (not leap) into the interval.

**4-part check order (6 pairs):** S/B → S/T → S/A → T/B → T/A → A/B. Outer pair S/B is highest priority.

### Doubling Rules (Root Position)

1. **Root is usually doubled** — in bass plus one upper voice.
2. ⚡ **Leading tone (7̂) is NEVER doubled** — too strong a tendency tone.
3. The 5th may be omitted; the final I chord may have a tripled root.
4. **Deceptive cadence (V→vi):** doubled 3rd in vi is correct. Both soprano and bass move parallel up to tonic with the leading tone; the other voices step down → 3rd doubled.

## 25. Part Writing by Root Relationship [Kostka/Payne Ch. 5–6]

Four cases cover every chord-to-chord move. Pick the case by interval between the two roots; the case dictates which voices hold, step, or leap.

### Case 1 — Repeated Root

Upper voices may arpeggiate freely. Observe spacing (§ 24).

### Case 2 — Roots P4 / P5 Apart (most fundamental tonal motion)

Three methods for 4-part writing:

- **Method 1.** Keep the common tone in the same voice (held). The other two upper voices step in the same direction.
- **Method 2.** All upper voices move in the same direction, no leap > a 3rd. (Leading tone in an inner voice may leap down to 5̂.)
- **Method 3.** Keep the common tone. The voice holding the 3rd of chord 1 leaps to the 3rd of chord 2. The remaining voice steps. (Leading tone in an inner voice may leap up to 3̂.)

### Case 3 — Roots a 3rd / 6th Apart

Two common pitch classes. Hold both common tones; only one voice needs to step.
- Step UP for descending-3rd root movement.
- Step DOWN for ascending-3rd root movement.

⚡ **Warning:** for ascending root movement by 3rd, do NOT omit the 5th of the second chord — the listener will hear the same chord, not a new one.

### Case 4 — Roots a 2nd / 7th Apart

No common pitch classes — all parts must move.
- Bass moves UP by step → upper voices move DOWN to nearest chord tone.
- Bass moves DOWN by step → upper voices move UP to nearest chord tone.

(Contrary motion: bass vs. upper voices.)

### Deceptive Progression V→vi — Special Case

- **Leading tone (7̂) in soprano / outer voice:** resolves UP to 1̂ (parallel with bass direction).
- **Other upper voices** step DOWN (contrary) to next available chord tones.
- **Result:** DOUBLED 3RD in vi (not the usual doubled root).
- **Leading tone in INNER voice in major:** MAY step down to 6̂ (avoids the parallel-with-bass obligation in an inner voice).
- ⚡ **Leading tone in INNER voice in MINOR (V→VI):** MUST resolve UP to 1̂ — stepping down creates an augmented 2nd (+2), which is FORBIDDEN.

### Voice Leading Decision Tree

```
Root relationship?
├── Same root       → arpeggiate freely, maintain spacing
├── P4/P5 apart     → Method 1, 2, or 3 (hold common tone preferred)
│                      7̂ in soprano: must resolve to 1̂
│                      7̂ in inner voice: may leap to 5̂ or 3̂
├── 3rd/6th apart   → hold 2 common tones, 1 voice steps
└── 2nd/7th apart   → all voices contrary to bass direction
```

### Tendency Tone Summary

| Tone | Normal resolution | Exception |
|---|---|---|
| 7̂ (leading tone) | Up to 1̂ | Inner voice only: may leap down to 5̂ or up to 3̂ |
| 4̂ | Down to 3̂ | Less obligatory |
| 7̂ in minor inner voice with V–VI | Must go up to 1̂ | Cannot descend (creates augmented 2nd) |

### DSP Proxy (our application)

Voice leading rules apply at the **variation decision level** (amt 2–5 chord ops):

- **Part writing Case 2 (P4/P5 roots, Method 1)** → `sequence` operation: move all chord tones as a unit.
- **Doubling rule (root doubled)** → our chord detection already assumes root in bass; respect this in semitone-shift ops.
- **Tendency tone 7̂→1̂** → do NOT pitch-shift the leading-tone slice DOWN across a half-cadence; it must resolve upward.
- **Deceptive resolution** → `deceptive_resolution` operation: shift final slice +2 or −3 semitones (existing amt 2 operation).
