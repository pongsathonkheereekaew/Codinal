# 09 — Workflow (Hanes)

1. **Full song listen** — identify direction, what to emulate from rough mix, what to improve
2. **Organize + clean** — label tracks, order (drums/perc/bass/guitars/keys/synths/BGV/adlibs/lead vox), clean clicks/pops/breaths on vocals
3. **Rough balance** — drums at top, get everything visible and at ballpark levels
4. **Vocals first** — get vocals sitting right *before* mixing instruments. Always mix with vocals on
5. **Mix instruments** with vocals in the mix — not an instrumental that gets vocals dropped in later
6. **Walk away** — return with fresh ears; first 30 minutes most productive, recreate that repeatedly
7. **Listen soft** — can you hear and understand the words? If not: vocal too low or muddy
8. **Listen distracted** — play mix while doing something else; what sticks out? What's missing?
9. **Vary volume** — listen loud (chest-thump check), listen soft (clarity check), don't burn hearing

## Master Bus Chain

Goes on at session start, mix *through* it. "Set and forget" but can adjust global EQ/comp/limiting as needed.

## Vocal Cleaning (Hanes Protocol)

- RX Mouth DeClick on mouth noises (select ~1s, zap)
- RX DeClick (multiband) for harder clicks
- Gain (-6 to -9dB) on big breaths
- High-pass filter before compressor: ~150–200Hz
- BGV: mute breaths entirely, fade in on attacks; time stacking so L/R start/end together
- Lead de-ess: CLA-76 then DeEsser @5500Hz ~-6dB, then manual riding for remaining outliers

**Vocal chain order:** Comp → EQ → De-esser

## Loudness Targets at Mix Stage

- DK Audio Meter (NBC/AES full-scale): loudest part sitting ~-6, peaks to -4
- Waves WLM shows ~-6 LUFS at loudest (hot mix)
- Master bus: light touches — bypass all and it's mostly a loudness difference, not a tone difference
- Leave very little for mastering engineer; goal = "sounds like it's already mastered"

## Monitoring

- Use mid-level speakers you know intimately — not top-shelf mastering monitors
- Mix on what sounds good on many systems, not just one great system
- Same speaker/room/amp for 17–25 years → muscle memory more valuable than "better" gear
- Passive speakers: no protection limiting your ability to hear problems
