# 03 — Dimension: Reverb, Delay, Chorus (Owsinski)

## 4 Reasons to Add Dimension

1. Create an aural space (recreate acoustic environment)
2. Add excitement / personality
3. Make bigger, wider, or deeper
4. Push a track further back in the mix

## Reverb Rules

- Smaller reverbs / short decays (<1s) make things sound *bigger* without pushing back
- Long decays + loud level = distant; short decay + loud level = big
- Reverb pre-delay timed to tempo keeps source dry/present
- Time reverb decay to die by the next snare hit
- Reverb causes massive masking — EQ to minimize: darken to tuck, brighten to feature
- Use one long reverb across major elements to tie the mix together
- Return reverb in mono + pan accordingly rather than always stereo

## Delay Rules

- Quarter note delay (ms) = 60,000 ÷ BPM
- Half note = ÷2; eighth note = ÷4; dotted = ×1.5; triplet = ×0.667
- Delay timed to tempo → blends, adds depth without being heard
- Delay NOT timed → sticks out (sometimes intentional)
- 25ms L + 50ms R = classic LA session guitar wide trick
- Guitar fattening: 12ms delay hard-panned opposite = two players in sync

## Sonic Layering

Give each instrument its own distinct ambient environment, just as each instrument has its own frequency range. Sonic atmospheres must not clash.

## Reverb Layering Psychology (Owsinski)

Depth in a mix is built by **distinct reverb environments per instrument**, not by louder/softer single reverb. This is the basis of the London perspective style.

### Layering order by brightness / decay

| Layer | Reverb type | Decay | EQ | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (back) | Plate or hall | Long (2–4 s) | Brightest (open ≥ 8 kHz) | Drum room glue, choir tail, "sky" |
| 2 (mid-back) | Chamber | Medium (1.5–2 s) | Mid-bright | Lead vocal, strings |
| 3 (mid-front) | Room | Short (0.5–1 s) | Mid-dark | Snare, guitar, keys |
| 4 (front) | Slap / short room | Very short (50–200 ms) | Darkest | Tightening transients, kick, bass tail |

⚡ **Rule:** brightest reverb has the longest decay, darkest has the shortest. Reverse this and the mix collapses front-to-back depth.

### London perspective — distinct environment per instrument

Instead of one master reverb send, give each lead element its **own** reverb (different model, different decay, different EQ). Listener perceives each element in a separate acoustic space → 3D depth without level changes.

- Lead vocal → plate, 1.8 s, EQ open
- Acoustic guitar → small room, 0.6 s, EQ dark
- Snare → 0.3 s plate, brightened
- Pad → 4 s hall, very dark

⚡ Cost: more bus tracks + CPU. Reward: dimension you can't get from a single send.

### Pre-delay sync

Pre-delay timed to song division (16th = 60000 / BPM / 4) keeps source dry/present, then reverb tail arrives on-grid → blends.
