# 10 — Genre-Specific Mixing

Mix decisions are genre-shaped. Same instrument, different rules.

## Hip-Hop / Trap / Drill

- **808** owns the sub — sine-wave fundamental at 40–60Hz; mono below ~80Hz; often on its own bus
- **Kick** lives at 80–120Hz thump + 3–5kHz click; HPF kick at ~80Hz so 808 owns sub
- **808 ducks to kick** — sidechain comp on 808, key=kick, fast attack 0ms, release tuned so 808 recovers before next kick
- **Sample-based** — beats often already mixed (loops, kits) → mix engineer balances vocal *to* the beat, not the other way
- **Vocal** sits *in* the beat, not above; doubles + adlibs panned wide; ad-libs heavily processed (throws, filters, FX)
- **Reverb sparse** on lead vocal; tight delays and throws preferred for space
- **Saturation** on 808 to add harmonics audible on phones/laptops (sub fundamental inaudible on small speakers)

## EDM / Electronic

- **Sidechain pump is aesthetic** — kick→bass, kick→pad, kick→master often pumped audibly; the pump *is* the groove
- **Loud uniform mix** — competition for ear at similar level; less dynamic variation than acoustic mixes
- **White noise sweeps / risers** as transitional FX; automate filter + reverb send through builds
- **Transient-heavy** — kick + snare + clap stacked for impact; layered hats
- **Sub mono, mids wide, highs ultra-wide** — exaggerated stereo image; stereo wideners common on pads/leads
- **All elements compete at similar level** — careful EQ carving (bass HPF kick range, lead synth notches around vocal pocket)
- **Master/mix bus** sidechained to kick for "breathing" pump even on full track

## Metal

- **Rhythm guitar double-tracked** — two takes, hard L + hard R 100% pan (quad-tracking = 4 takes, pairs at 100% L/R); creates wide guitar wall, dry center for vox
- **Scooped mids** — cut 300–800Hz on rhythm gtr to clear vocal/snare space (less universal in modern metal; surgical cuts often preferred over wide scoop)
- **Kick click** — boost 3–5kHz heavily for click presence cutting through guitar wall; sub-kick fundamental 60–80Hz
- **Parallel drum compression heavy** — crushed parallel bus blended underneath for size; close to NY trick but with more aggression
- **Bass DI + amp blended** — DI for articulation/attack, amp for grit; HPF guitars at 80–120Hz so bass owns low-mid
- **Lead vocal less reverb** than rock — clarity over ambience; aggressive sibilance control
- **Cymbals/overheads** less prominent than rock — they get buried by guitar wall; high-shelf boost 8–12kHz on OH for air

## Jazz / Acoustic / Classical

- **Minimal processing philosophy** — preserve performance dynamics; compression light or absent
- **Room mics blended** — natural ambience > artificial reverb; sometimes single-mic or stereo-pair sufficient
- **Natural dynamics preserved** — verses *should* be quieter than choruses; no aggressive automation
- **Warmth over brightness** — gentle high-shelf cut, low-mid presence; resist hyped 10kHz boost
- **Wide stereo from real positions** — drum kit imaged from drummer's POV (OH spread), piano stereo'd from natural pickup spacing; no artificial wideners
- **Compression** only for level consistency, not effect — 1–2dB GR max on bass/vocal; rhythm section often uncompressed
- **EQ subtractive** — small cuts to clean mud (200–400Hz on close mics) rather than boosts
- **Reverb plate/hall** matching real-world room; sparse and tasteful, never washy
- **Mix bus** ultra-light — 0.5–1dB glue comp max, often no comp at all on jazz
