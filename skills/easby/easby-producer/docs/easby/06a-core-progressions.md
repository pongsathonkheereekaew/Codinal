## 1. Cadences — How Phrases End

A cadence is the punctuation. The same 4 chords ending two different ways tell two different stories.

| Cadence | Motion | Feel | When I reach for it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Authentic (PAC)** | V → I, both root position, melody on tonic | Definitive period. Lights up, scene ends. | End of a chorus, end of a track. The "we're done" cadence. |
| **Imperfect Authentic (IAC)** | V → I but inverted or melody not on tonic | Resolved but soft. Comma, not period. | End of a verse going into pre-chorus. Closure without commitment. |
| **Plagal** | IV → I | Amen. Settled, warm, churchy. | Outros, fade-outs, gospel/soul tags. Less final than PAC — more like exhaling. |
| **Deceptive** | V → vi (or V → bVI in minor) | The expected chord didn't show up. | ⚡ Best move in the book for extending a phrase. The ear is set up for I, vi catches it. Buys 4 more bars. |
| **Half** | Any → V, ends ON the dominant | Suspended. Question mark. | End of a verse, transition to bridge. The bar line that begs the next section to start. |

**Creative use:** stack two cadences against each other in an 8-bar loop. Bars 1–4 land on a half cadence (question), bars 5–8 land on PAC (answer). That's a complete musical sentence in 8 bars without writing more notes.

## 2. Seventh Chords — Colour Without Cost

Adding a 7th to a triad doesn't change the chord's function. It changes its temperature.

| Quality | Symbol | Built from | What I hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Major 7** | maj7, M7 | Major triad + major 7 | Lush, suspended-in-amber. Bossa, lo-fi, anything pretty. The chord that says "expensive." |
| **Dominant 7** | 7 | Major triad + minor 7 | Tension that wants to move. Blues, funk, the engine of every authentic cadence. |
| **Minor 7** | m7, min7 | Minor triad + minor 7 | Smooth, jazzy, no drama. Neo-soul default. Less sad than plain minor — almost wistful. |
| **Half-diminished** | m7b5, ø7 | Diminished triad + minor 7 | Anxious but holding it together. The ii in minor keys. Last-call-at-the-bar energy. |
| **Fully diminished** | dim7, °7 | Diminished triad + diminished 7 | All tritones, no home. Pure transit. One bar max, then resolve. |

⚡ **Producer trick:** swap any triad for its 7th version mid-loop. Same function, instant sophistication. C → Cmaj7 → C is a one-finger variation that reads as "second time around."

## 3. Harmonic Progressions — The Highways

### ⚡ Circle of Fifths

Root motion down a 5th (or up a 4th) is the strongest pull in tonal music. Every authentic cadence is one step of this. Chain them and you get the engine of Autumn Leaves, Fly Me to the Moon, half of pop music's bridges.

In C: `Cmaj7 → Fmaj7 → Bm7b5 → Em7 → Am7 → Dm7 → G7 → Cmaj7`

Use it when a section needs to keep moving without changing key. Each chord is the V (or ii-V) of the next. Forward momentum, zero thinking.

### ⚡ I–V–vi–IV (and Its Rotations)

The pop progression. Same 4 chords, 4 rotations, completely different vibes:

| Rotation | Vibe | Famous example |
|---|---|---|
| **I–V–vi–IV** | Anthemic, uplifting | "Let It Be," "Don't Stop Believin'" |
| **vi–IV–I–V** | Bittersweet, sad bop | "Apologize," "Numb" |
| **IV–I–V–vi** | Rising, hopeful | "Umbrella" |
| **I–vi–IV–V** | 50s doo-wop nostalgia | "Stand By Me" |

⚡ **Same chords, different downbeat → different song.** When the loop is fine but the mood is wrong, rotate the start point before touching the chords.

### Predominant Function Family (Kostka/Payne Ch.7, Ch.18)

The chords that *prepare* the V (and therefore the V → I authentic cadence) form a family. They are functionally substitutable; pick by voice-leading taste and bass-line preference.

| Chord | Pre-dominant strength | Bass note (in C) | When to reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ii** (Dm) | Strong, smooth voice-leading | D | Default pre-V — minimal voice motion |
| **ii6** (Dm/F) | Strong, with stepwise bass into V | F → G | Bass step-down approach to V |
| **ii6/5** (Dm7/F) | Strong + colour (added 7th) | F → G | Jazz/pop default — adds 7 to the texture |
| **IV** (F) | Classic, more "open" than ii | F | Hymn / folk / classic-rock setting |
| **bII** (Db Neapolitan) | Heavy chromatic | Db → G or F | Dramatic pre-V — see `06b` §9 |
| **vi** (Am) | Weakest pre-V (more like tonic-substitute) | A | Bridge into ii or IV (vi → ii → V) |

⚡ **ii6/5 vs IV substitution rule.** ii6/5 keeps a 5th in the bass moving up by step (F → G); IV jumps a 5th down to V root motion. Both work — ii6/5 reads smoother for slow ballads, IV reads more declarative for anthems. When in doubt, ii6/5 for low-amt variations (texture-preserving) and IV for high-amt (clearer mood swing).

### Harmonic Rhythm (Kostka/Payne Ch.7, Ch.10)

The *rate* at which chords change. Independent of melody rhythm.

| Harmonic rhythm | Chords change every | Feel | Easby use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Very slow** | 2–4 bars | Cinematic, drone, ambient | High-amt variations get more room — sequence operations land harder |
| **Slow** | 1 bar | Pop verse default | amt 1–3 ops one-per-bar |
| **Medium** | Half bar (2 beats) | Pop chorus, rock | amt 1–2 ops only — listener already tracking change |
| **Fast** | Beat-level | Jazz, swing, bebop | Refuse most amt 3+ ops — too much going on already |
| **Variable** | Mixed | Through-composed, song with pre-chorus lift | Apply amt density to slowest section |

⚡ **Rule:** Easby reduces amt by one rung when source harmonic rhythm is "medium" or faster. Adding more change to an already-busy progression = noise.

### Sequence Types Beyond Simple Step

`06a` §5 covers the basic sequence (repeat motif up a scale degree). Kostka/Payne Ch.7 extends:

| Sequence type | Pattern | Easby DSP proxy |
|---|---|---|
| **Descending 5ths chain** (D-5) | Each chord's root is a 5th below the previous (V7 → I → IV → vii° → iii → vi → ii → V) | Pitch-shift each chord slice −5 st (or +7 st) per step |
| **Ascending 2nds with secondary dominants** | V/ii → ii → V/iii → iii → V/IV → IV | Insert a dominant stamp +2 st before each diatonic chord (see `06b` §6) |
| **Descending 2nds (3rd-related)** | I → vii° → vi → V (Pachelbel descent) | Pitch-shift slice −2 st per step, alternating chord-quality |
| **Ascending 5ths** | I → V → ii → vi (less common) | +7 st per step, rare in pop |

⚡ **Sequence anchor:** minimum 2 sequential units required before listener perceives the pattern. Easby emits sequences with ≥2 transposed copies of the same shape, then optionally a "surprise" 3rd unit that breaks the pattern (deceptive resolution).

### bVII Rock Patterns

In a major key, borrowing bVII from the parallel minor gives you the rock/cinematic move:

- **I–bVII–IV** ("Sweet Home Alabama," "Sympathy for the Devil") — the rock cliché in the best way
- **i–bVII–bVI–bVII** (minor) — Andalusian descent, flamenco-into-metal
- **I–bVII–bVI–V** — minor-key drama in a major key

The bVII bypasses the leading tone, which is exactly why rock loves it — no classical politeness, just power.

## 4. Non-Chord Tones — The Embellishments

A non-chord tone (NCT) is a melody note that doesn't belong to the current chord. How it's approached and left determines what kind of NCT it is — and whether it sounds intentional or sloppy.

| Type | Approach | Leave | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Passing tone** | Step | Step (same direction) | Connects two chord tones. Invisible glue. |
| **Neighbor tone** | Step | Step (opposite direction) | Decorates a single chord tone. Ornament. |
| **Appoggiatura** | Leap | Step (opposite direction) | Strong-beat dissonance that begs to resolve. Emotional. |
| **Escape tone** | Step | Leap (opposite direction) | Weak-beat sigh. Throws the ear away from resolution. |
| **Suspension** | Same pitch (tied over) | Step down | The classic 4-3, 7-6, 9-8. Pure tension-release. |
| **Retardation** | Same pitch (tied over) | Step up | Upside-down suspension. Less common, more hopeful. |
| **Anticipation** | Step | Same pitch | Early arrival of the next chord tone. Eager. |
| **Pedal point** | Sustained | Sustained | One note held while harmony moves over/under it. Trance, drones, V-pedal builds. |
| **Neighbor group / Cambiata** | Mixed step+leap pattern | Returns to original | Two NCTs in a row that frame a chord tone. Baroque ornament. |

### ⚡ The ≤ M3 Gap Rule

When inserting an NCT into a slice (passing, neighbor, escape), the interval between adjacent chord tones must be **≤ a major 3rd (4 semitones)**. Wider than that and the "passing" note sounds like a wrong note, not a passing one.

- C → E (M3, 4 semitones) — passing tone D ✓
- C → G (P5, 7 semitones) — passing tones D and E start to sound like a new melody, not an ornament ✗

This is non-negotiable. Violate it and the embellishment reads as a generation error.

## 5. Melodic Alteration Toolkit

Nine ways to transform a motif. Not all of them survive contact with tonal pop music.

| Technique | Operation | Useful? |
|---|---|---|
| **Sequence** | Repeat motif starting on a different scale degree | ⚡ Always. Cheapest way to extend a hook. |
| **Inversion** | Flip contour: up becomes down, by interval | ⚡ Works great. Same notes, fresh angle. |
| **Augmentation** | Stretch durations 2× or 4× | ⚡ Climaxes, codas. Makes a phrase feel epic. |
| **Diminution** | Compress durations ÷2 or ÷4 | ⚡ Fills, energy ramps, double-time feel. |
| **Retrograde** | Play phrase backwards | ✗ Rarely works in tonal music. Cadential motion is asymmetric — running it backwards sounds like a glitch. Reserve for ambient/textural moments only. (06 is canonical on this — overrides 03.) |
| **Fragmentation** | Take 2–3 notes, develop alone | Strong. Verse-from-chorus, breakdowns. |
| **Truncation** | Cut the tail | Useful. Question-mark effect. |
| **Extension** | Add a tail | Useful. Phrase outgrows itself. |
| **Interpolation** | Insert new material mid-phrase | Risky. Easy to lose the original's identity. |

⚡ Default move when "make it different" is the brief: **inversion + sequence on the second half**. Identity preserved, ear refreshed. (Easby gates inversion on pitch confidence ≥ 0.7 — see `06d-dsp-wiring.md` § Pitch confidence gate.)
